What is Market Basket Analysis?

Market Basket Analysis (MBA) is a data mining technique that finds products or items that frequently appear together in the same transaction. It uses the Apriori algorithm to discover association rules — patterns of the form eggs & milk → bread — meaning "customers who buy eggs and milk also tend to buy bread."

These rules are used in retail for cross-selling, shelf placement, promotions, and inventory planning. Each rule is evaluated with several statistical metrics so you can judge how strong and reliable the association actually is.

Parameter Guide

Minimum Support (%)
How common must an itemset be across all transactions?

Support measures what fraction of transactions contain a given set of items. A support of 5% means the itemset appears in at least 5 out of every 100 transactions.

  • Lower → more rules found, slower analysis
  • Higher → only the most common patterns
  • Typical starting range: 2% – 10%
Minimum Confidence (%)
How reliable must a rule be?

Confidence is the probability that the consequent (right side) is bought given the antecedent (left side) is already in the basket. A confidence of 70% means: in 70% of transactions containing the antecedent, the consequent also appears.

  • Lower → more rules, including weak ones
  • Higher → only highly reliable rules
  • Typical starting range: 50% – 80%

Understanding the Output Metrics

Each rule has the form Antecedent → Consequent. The table on the results page shows these metrics for every rule:

Support Fraction of all transactions that contain both sides of the rule. Higher = more common pattern.
Confidence Probability the consequent is bought when the antecedent is bought. The mini-bar in the results table shows this visually.
Lift How much more likely the consequent is bought with the antecedent vs. at random.
Lift > 1 = positive association  |  Lift = 1 = no association  |  Lift < 1 = items avoid each other. Aim for Lift ≥ 1.5 for actionable rules.
Leverage Co-occurrence above what you'd expect by chance. Positive = they appear together more than random. Ranges roughly -0.25 to 0.25.
Conviction Strength of the directional dependency. Values above 1 indicate the rule is not a coincidence. Very high conviction (e.g., >3) = very strong rule.
Zhang's Metric Balanced score from -1 to 1. Positive = items co-occur more than expected. Negative = items avoid each other. Near 0 = independent.
Quick tip: Start with Support 2–5% and Confidence 50% to see if rules exist, then raise thresholds to filter for quality. Focus on rules where Lift ≥ 1.5 — those are the genuinely useful associations.
